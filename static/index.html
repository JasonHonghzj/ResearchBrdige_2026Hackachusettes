Why this hole is interesting

An empty cell on its own means nothing. This one is ranked because two literatures that never cite each other both run through the same mechanistic terms — Swanson’s ABC pattern, with the B-terms restricted to substances and physiological processes.

Source abstract — method half

Patients undergoing upper gastrointestinal endoscopy provided exhaled breath samples analysed by selected-ion flow-tube mass spectrometry. Exhaled volatile organic compound profiles discriminated gastric and oesophageal cancer from benign disease with an area under the curve of 0.85. No pancreatic cases were included in this cohort, and no multi-centre validation has been undertaken.

The third claim fails on two counts: the cohort contains no pancreatic cases, and the abstract states explicitly that no multi-centre validation exists. Nothing in either source supports it.

And what it doesn’t. Raise the bar to three or more holdout papers and the signal disappears — 6 of 328 flagged, which the matched control matches. So this ranks candidates roughly twice as well as chance. It does not predict breakthroughs, and we don’t claim it does.

Prior art worth naming: Swanson & Smalheiser’s Arrowsmith has done literature-based discovery since the 1990s. What’s new here is the measured lift, the per-claim verification, and the handoff to named local labs.

Opportunity brief · early diagnostics · example

Breath-based volatile profiling for pancreatic cancer detection

The gap

Exhaled volatile organic compound analysis is an active method in upper-gastrointestinal cancer detection, with 96 papers up to 2018. Pancreatic neoplasms carry 214 early-detection papers over the same period. No paper in the training corpus does both, yet the two literatures share four mechanistic terms — polyamines, volatile organic compounds, exhaled breath condensate, and GC-MS.

Evidence that survived checking

Two of three drafted claims were supported by their cited abstracts and are reproduced with sources. One claim — that breath testing has been validated for pancreatic screening in a multi-centre trial — was deleted; the cited cohort contained no pancreatic cases and the abstract states no such validation exists.

Confidence

Gaps ranked this way are filled in later literature about twice as often as comparable empty cells. This is a prioritisation signal, not a prediction. Treat the brief as a starting hypothesis to take to a specialist, not a finding.

Draft first message

Subject: VOC breath profiling for pancreatic early detection — possible collaboration Dear Dr. ———, Your group's work on exhaled volatile profiling in upper-GI cancer detection caught my attention while I was mapping early-diagnostics literature in Massachusetts. The mapping turned up something I wanted to put to you: the VOC-profiling and pancreatic early-detection literatures share four mechanistic terms — polyamines, VOCs, exhaled breath condensate and GC-MS — but I could not find a paper that applies the method to the problem. Two groups at Dana-Farber and UMass Chan hold pancreatic cohorts. If the absence reflects an untried combination rather than a known dead end, I would value fifteen minutes to hear why. I've attached the two-page brief with sources, including one claim our check removed as unsupported.
