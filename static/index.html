How a source check could work

Illustrative walkthrough · scripted outcomes

This fixed example illustrates accepting and removing claims. Its text and results are predefined; it does not run a verifier or feed the research brief.

Example claims

· Exhaled VOC profiles separated upper-GI cancer from benign disease with an AUC of 0.85.
· Polyamines were elevated in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma versus chronic pancreatitis.
· Breath testing has been validated for pancreatic cancer screening in a multi-centre trial.
2 of 3 claims supported. Illustration complete · 1 claim removed.

Illustrative source text

Patients undergoing upper gastrointestinal endoscopy provided exhaled breath samples analysed by selected-ion flow-tube mass spectrometry. Exhaled volatile organic compound profiles discriminated gastric and oesophageal cancer from benign disease with an area under the curve of 0.85. No pancreatic cases were included in this cohort, and no multi-centre validation has been undertaken.
Second illustrative source: Polyamines were elevated in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma versus chronic pancreatitis.

The third claim fails on two counts: the cohort contains no pancreatic cases, and the abstract states explicitly that no multi-centre validation exists. Nothing in either source supports it.

Reading the results

A pairing is a candidate when its MeSH search returns at most two early-detection papers. A paper count shows indexed use, not that a method is effective. MeSH coverage, indexing delays, synonyms and the search definition can all affect these counts.

The map uses saved counts; Check can query PubMed. The Library searches a separate 60-paper starter set and includes a functional literal source checker.
